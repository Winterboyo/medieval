# Medieval Siege — game design (working v6, 2026-10-03)

This is the target game design for the two-team pivot. `AGENTS.md` holds working rules, the art pipeline, and tool instructions; `layout.json` v2.1 holds the approved exterior map and mirrored castle interior coordinates. `CASTLE-INTERIOR-V2-PROPOSAL.md` records the approved interior measurements and route reasoning. The repository shows implementation progress, not design authority. `CLAUDE.md` and `README.md` contain older descriptions and must not override this design. A change to this document does not silently change `layout.json` or the game code.

**Status words:** **Decided** means the user chose it. **Placeholder** is a number or simple behavior to test and tune. **Open** requires a user answer before dependent gameplay or art is built. **Parked** means preserve existing work but do not extend it. The open list at the end is part of the spec, not permission for an agent to invent answers.

## Core concept

Two-team medieval siege on one static island. Red and Blue defend mirrored, pre-built castles. Players gather resources, bring them home, improve their team's equipment and defenses, then assault the enemy Keep. Combat is between players. A match is short and resets for the next queue. There are no AI troop regiments, player-built bases, persistent world, or cross-match resource losses. Raids can steal resources, while destroying the Keep wins the match.

## Art direction (decided)
- **Decided 2026-10-03 (supersedes the two lines marked below):** the look moves toward *The Forge*'s textured, low-key style: low-poly shapes kept, every surface textured, Roblox Terrain for the ground, darker stone and wood, overcast daylight and firelit interiors (AGENTS.md §9, REALISM-TARGET.md). Map, layout and gameplay are unchanged.
- *(Superseded 2026-10-03, see above.)* Overall style: stylized low-poly — chunky, faceted geometry with flat angled surfaces, not smooth/rounded default Roblox shapes. Exaggerated proportions, saturated flat color separation between materials. Closer to a fantasy-medieval low-poly asset-pack look than realistic or default-Studio terrain.
- Resource node identity follows Catan's approach: ground material can stay simple and consistent — a node's identity comes from a distinct built prop scene sitting on top of it, not from ground color alone:
  - **Wood** — a cluster of low-poly trees with chunky faceted round canopies. These are the ONLY round-canopy trees on the map; decorative trees are stacked-cone pines.
  - **Stone** — a jagged angular rock outcropping, distinctly lighter/grayer than Ore so the two read as different at a glance.
  - **Ore** — a mine entrance or exposed vein: dark rock with small embedded glinting chunks.
  - **Grain (parked)** — a golden wheat-stalk cluster or a strongly colored field patch if a later recipe justifies adding it. It is not a first-playable resource node.
  - **Wool** — a small fenced pasture with a few sheep models scattered in it.
- Castle: use the reference family and asset contract in `AGENTS.md` §§9–12. Round towers are faceted cylinders and cones, scaled to a 5-stud avatar. The current castle kit is a prototype; new Keep damage states, tunnel pieces, and any weapon change need their own approved asset tasks.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — recipe-to-model rule:** crafted equipment visibly uses the materials in its recipe. For example, the crossbow shows a wooden stock, iron mechanism, and wool component; the ram shows its timber frame and log, stone ballast, wool canopy, and iron striking head/brackets. Show each ingredient clearly without making the silhouette hard to read. The Stone-and-Wool bow needs a reviewed visual construction before its asset is built.
- **Decided 2026-10-01:** inside the castle walls, use a compact village-like arrangement of distinct small working buildings around paths and a courtyard. The user's Minecraft-village comparison describes the spatial feel, not art or trading mechanics to copy. The blacksmith's forge replaces the single shared workshop in the earlier interior sketch. `layout.json` v2.1 contains approved mirrored positions for the Keep and its stockpile room, barracks, four specialist buildings, ram pad, tower access, and ballista mount; `CASTLE-INTERIOR-V2-PROPOSAL.md` explains their measurements. New interior art still needs its own Blender asset tasks and visual review.
- *(Superseded 2026-10-03: ground moves to Roblox Terrain.)* **Ground terrain is part of the style, not just props.** Build it in Blender from `layout.json` and import static FBX tiles: a green island, faceted shoreline, and sea outside the playable area. Avoid Roblox SmoothTerrain for this look.

## Battlefield composition (approved exterior and interior in `layout.json` v2.1)

- Use `concepts/roman-battlefield-mirrored-castles.png` as a composition reference. The approved `layout.json` v2.1 has prominent 183.6 × 183.6 castle rings. Their vertical silhouette must grow with their footprint so the towers do not become visually squat. Preserve enough open meadow around the road for meaningful gathering on home, middle, forward, and shared resource nodes. `MAP-REVISION-PROPOSAL.md` records why these exterior positions were chosen; judge the actual look in-game.
- One readable dirt road connects the two facing gates through the middle. Its painted/dirt edges may meander gently, while the traversable center line stays clear. Resource pads, trees, and fixed cover do not block the road or gate approaches.
- Shallow craters and small barricades are permanent battlefield cover. Players can crouch behind crater rims and barricades; each must provide actual sightline protection at avatar scale. Place them in mirrored pairs beside the road, with clear routes around them. Their exact footprints, heights, and positions belong in the revised layout proposal.
- The book-inspired battle scene is visual direction for terrain, castle presence, and atmosphere. Armies, elephant, and water cannon in the concept image are not automatically game assets or match mechanics.

## Match contract

- **Decided:** two fixed teams, Red and Blue; no player elimination or friendly fire. Players respawn on their own side after being knocked down. The pre-built castles and map reset between matches. Team size is **open** (6v6 placeholder).
- **Decided:** the terrain and castle positions are mirrored across `layout.json`'s x = 0 axis. The map stays static. Resource slots are mirrored; varying their active types per match is a later task.
- **Decided:** a team wins when the enemy Keep reaches 0 HP. The Keep can be damaged whenever an attacker reaches it; a gate or wall breach is not a prerequisite. Looting is a separate goal, not a win condition. A win ends the match and returns players to the lobby for the next match.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — no preparation phase (supersedes the 2026-10-01 choice):** when the lobby ends and the match starts, both teams can move across the full playable map, fight, damage structures, gather, carry, craft, deposit, repair, and contribute to the ram immediately. Home, forward, and shared-center resource areas are available from match start. There is no protected opening, temporary side boundary, or later combat-unlock transition. The 15-second lobby is a queue/reset interval, not a gathering phase.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — starting and respawn equipment:** every player starts with free basic Axe, Pickaxe, and Shears, and has those tools restored without duplicates after knockdown. These tools can deal weak melee damage so an uncrafted player can defend themself; their exact damage is **open** but must stay below the crafted Wood Sword's combat value. Players start with no sword, armor, bow, crossbow, ammunition, or carried materials. The Wood Sword is the first crafted combat weapon. Completed personal gear and items, including crafted weapons, armor, unused ammunition, and bandages, remain with their owner through knockdown; only carried crafting materials enter the fallen cargo bag. Gear resets with the match, not with each knockdown.
- **Placeholder:** 12–15 minute hard cap (the current code uses 13 minutes), 15-second lobby, 8-second knockdown. At timeout, compare remaining Keep HP, then total wall HP; an exact tie is a draw. Confirm this tiebreaker before implementing it against the new Keep.
- **Open:** final team size; matchmaking and party behavior. `layout.json` has no shrinking zone. The old free-for-all zone is parked unless the user explicitly restores it.

## Resources and team economy

- **Decided 2026-10-03 — first-playable gathered resources:** Wood, Stone, Ore, and Wool. Grain is parked until it has a useful recipe; do not allocate it a live node slot just because old code or art exists. Smelted Iron is a crafted ingredient made from Ore at the Blacksmith, not a fifth field node. The old Timber, Ingot, and Tonic counters do not automatically enter the new economy. The approved `layout.json` still allows Grain in some mirrored resource slots; a separate approved layout revision must reassign their allowed types before four-resource node gameplay is generated. Raw resources and Smelted Iron can all be carried, deposited, and looted under the same bag and stockpile rules. Finished personal items do not enter fallen material bags; voluntary item transfer rules, if any, are **open**.
- **Decided:** gathering and foraging remain meaningful throughout the match, including while players fight and siege. The map must retain reachable home, middle, forward, and shared resource areas off the central road; all are accessible from match start. Nodes have finite supply, deplete when gathered, and regenerate on a curve that is fastest at moderate depletion. Mirrored slots give each side equal opportunities. Ore appears forward and in the shared center in the current approved layout. Ore mining takes longer than gathering the other raw resources so high-tier gear and ammunition cannot be produced routinely; the exact mining time is **open**. No movement-speed penalty tied to Ore has been chosen.
- **Decided 2026-10-01:** gathered goods go into the gathering player's carried cargo. The player chooses to take ingredients directly to a specialist for crafting or deposit them in the castle's pooled team stockpile. Deposit is a contribution, not a prerequisite for crafting. Carried goods form a bag immediately at the player's knockdown location. The current instant deposit code is a gap, not the desired behavior.
- **First-playtest material bag (2026-10-03 placeholders):** each player has one mixed bag with **24 load points**. Wood and Wool cost 1 point per unit; Stone costs 2; Ore and Smelted Iron cost 4 each. Load is the sum of quantity times per-unit cost, capped at 24. Gathering accepts only whole units that fit and depletes the node by exactly the accepted amount; Smelted Iron uses the same capacity after crafting. A bag with unused points may still be unable to fit a heavier resource. These weights and capacity are tunable, not final balance. Whether cargo changes movement speed is **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — equipment choice target, revised for continuous play:** an Iron sword, Iron armor, and a Crossbow with usable ammunition are competing high-tier personal goals. A typical player should face a meaningful choice about which to pursue first during the live match; this is a pacing target, not a hard equipment lock or a promise that only one can be made in a match. Wood and Stone sword tiers remain required steps toward the Iron sword; their exact recipe costs are still **open**. Bag loads limit each trip but do not by themselves limit total crafting. Ore mining time, smelting time, recipe quantities, and travel must be tuned together; those numbers remain **open** unless stated below.
- **Gather → carry → craft or deposit loop:** gathering removes raw goods from a finite node and adds the same amount to that player's carried cargo. Personal gear and consumables crafted at specialists spend only the initiating player's carried ingredients; they cannot draw from the pooled stockpile, so one player cannot spend teammates' deposits making personal gear. At the team's stockpile, offer both **Deposit all** (move every carried resource into the team pool) and **Choose goods and amounts** (move only the selected quantities, leaving the rest carried for personal crafting). Each deposit subtracts exactly what it credits; it does not sweep unselected goods. A teammate cannot withdraw deposited goods into personal cargo or use them for personal crafting. Shared repairs and castle defenses spend from the pooled stockpile through their own interactions. The team battering ram accepts reserved donations from either players' carried goods or the team stockpile; stockpile allocations never become personal cargo merely by entering that project. Enemy stockpile looting can also remove goods under the raid rules. A transfer or craft must never create resources through duplicate credit, death, or interrupted interaction. Deposit timing, shared-spending controls, recipe costs, crafted-item delivery, and player-facing prompts remain **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-01 — fallen cargo, clarified 2026-10-03:** at knockdown, transfer only the player's carried crafting materials exactly once into a bag at that position and clear their carried material cargo. Completed personal items remain with the player as specified above. The 30-second lifetime starts immediately; the later respawn does not create another bag or restart the timer. The bag stays there; nobody picks up or carries the bag object. Any player on either team may ransack its contents. Enemy players steal resources into their own carried bag; teammates recover them into theirs. Ransacked goods stay carried until that player crafts with or deposits them; they never credit a team automatically. The bag disappears when emptied or 30 seconds after knockdown, whichever comes first. Ransacking never refreshes its timer; resources left at expiry leave the match without crediting either team.
- **Decided 2026-10-01 — bag ransacking, revised for weighted load 2026-10-03:** if the total load of every resource remaining in the fallen bag fits in the player's free load points, offer **Take all**; one 2-second stationary hold transfers the entire contents. If it does not all fit, show the bag's resource types and quantities; the player selects which goods and amounts to take, up to their free load points, then completes one 2-second stationary hold. Do not automatically fill remaining load with unselected goods. Moving, taking damage, or bag expiry cancels the hold with no transfer. On completion, recheck bag contents and free load, transfer only selected goods still available and fitting, subtract exactly what was granted, and leave the rest in the bag. A player without enough free load for any remaining resource cannot ransack it. Concurrent completed holds cannot duplicate goods. Ore and Smelted Iron can both be stolen this way. This interaction is distinct from logarithmic **stockpile** looting. The 2-second hold is tunable after playtest; interaction range is **open**.
- **Decided:** resources earned in-match pay for repairs, gear upgrades, and siege equipment. Personal gear and consumables crafted by specialists use the crafter's carried ingredients. The team battering ram is funded by contributions from carried goods and/or the pooled team stockpile; shared repairs and castle defenses spend from that stockpile. Funding for any other future siege tool is **open**. Weapons cannot be bought with coins or other purchases. Bow and crossbow are the chosen ranged hand weapons. Costs, prerequisites outside the sword and armor upgrade chains, and item delivery remain **open** except where stated in the first-playable catalog below.
- **Decided 2026-10-01:** redesign stockpile looting without a one-time snapshot. The old 20% exposed-slice rule is superseded. Looting has diminishing returns: cumulative goods stolen should rise roughly logarithmically with sustained looting effort, so taking each later bundle of the same size requires more effort. A candidate tuning curve is `cumulative loot = K × ln(1 + effort / T)`; `K`, `T`, interaction pacing, and the unit of effort are **placeholders**, not approved balance. Loot must come from goods the defender actually holds, and every transfer subtracts exactly what it grants. A raider can loot by reaching the stockpile, including through the tunnel, without a gate or wall breach. Whether progress is shared by all raiders, whether loot must be carried out, and any protected reserve are **open**.

### First-playable crafting catalog (chosen 2026-10-03)

These are chosen outputs and required material types, not complete numeric recipes unless a quantity is shown. Personally crafted gear and consumables use only the crafter's carried ingredients. **Decided 2026-10-03:** smelting consumes exactly 1 carried Ore and produces exactly 1 carried Smelted Iron, with no additional input or yield multiplier. Both occupy 4 load points, so conversion does not alter bag load. Smelted Iron can be carried to the Fletcher or Carpenter, deposited in the team stockpile, recovered from a fallen bag, or stolen from an enemy bag or stockpile under the existing loot rules.

- **Blacksmith/forge:** smelt Ore into Iron. Craft Wood, Stone, and Iron swords as successive tiers; craft Wood, Stone, and Iron armor as successive tiers. **Decided 2026-10-03:** each chain starts with a crafted Wood item. Crafting Stone requires the owner's Wood item plus their carried ingredients, consumes the Wood item, and gives the Stone replacement. Crafting Iron similarly requires and consumes their Stone item; players cannot skip a tier or keep the consumed lower-tier item. The Iron sword requires Smelted Iron. Iron armor requires Smelted Iron and Wool; Wool is also required in the Wood and Stone armor recipes as visible padding. Other ingredients and exact counts for swords and armor are **open**.
- **Fletcher:** craft a Bow from Wool and Stone, and Bow Arrows from Wood and Stone. Craft a Crossbow from Wood, Wool, and Smelted Iron. Craft Crossbow Bolts and larger Ballista Bolts; Ballista Bolts require Smelted Iron. The other bolt ingredients, ingredient quantities, ammunition per craft, and how the Stone-and-Wool Bow is visibly constructed are **open**. A Wool-and-Wood Bolt Pouch is only a later proposal in `IDEAS.md`, not part of this chosen catalog.
- **Alchemist:** craft Bandages using Wool. Healing effect, timing, count per craft, and any additional ingredients are **open**.
- **Carpenter/team projects:** build the Battering Ram from 18 Wood, 8 Stone, 4 Wool, and 2 Smelted Iron. These are **first-playtest quantity placeholders accepted for the initial recipe**: 32 contributed material units, or 46 load points at the current weights. The ingredients fit within two 24-point material loads, though mining, smelting, node placement, and travel may require more than two journeys. Wood forms the chassis, wheels, and suspended log; Stone appears as low ballast; Wool forms a padded canopy; Iron forms the striking head and brackets. The ram uses the shared contribution and refund rules below. The fixed defensive Ballista is a team defense paid for from the team stockpile; its construction and repair ingredients and interaction are **open**, while its bolts are crafted by the Fletcher. Shared gate, wall, and tower repairs also spend the team stockpile; their exact recipes remain **open**.

## Castle and assault

- **Decided:** each castle has one mirrored wall ring with individually identified wall sections, four corner towers, one gate with a separate door, one team stockpile inside its Keep, and a damageable Keep. Banners recolor for Red and Blue. `layout.json` v2.1 holds the approved exterior and interior geometry: 183.6 × 183.6 ring, 35 wall sections, centers at `x = ±280`, facing gates at `x = ±188.2`, and a 44 × 44 Keep containing an 18 × 12 stockpile room. The old 140 × 140 free-for-all ring remains obsolete.
- **Decided:** defenders can stand on tower tops via stairs or ladders. Gate, wall sections, towers, and Keep have independent HP and intact, damaged, and rubble looks. Repairable structures consume team resources and show timber scaffolding. Keep HP, whether it can be repaired, and exact damage thresholds are **open**; its destruction ends the match.
- **Decided 2026-10-01, revised 2026-10-03:** the first playable siege includes a pre-built barracks and four crafting-only specialists in separate village-like buildings: blacksmith, fletcher, carpenter, and alchemist. The blacksmith's dedicated forge replaces the shared workshop in the earlier interior proposal and smelts Ore into Iron; the Fletcher makes the Bow, Crossbow, and ammunition; the Carpenter handles timber and siege projects; the Alchemist makes Bandages. Their chosen outputs and material requirements are in the catalog above. Ranks, specialist theft, negotiation, and Minecraft-style trading are not part of v1. The barracks' minimum function is team respawn with a courtyard exit; it adds no AI troop regiment. Unspecified quantities, crafting time, and interface remain **open**. The team stockpile is a distinct room inside the Keep, with deposit and loot interactions there. Reaching it, including via the tunnel, is enough to attempt looting; no wall or gate breach is required. Its room coordinates are approved in `layout.json` v2.1. Mason, cook, and weaver are later ideas in IDEAS.md, outside v1.
- **Gate route:** the widest entrance, opened by heavy player hits or a battering ram. The ram is optional, but the design target is for teams to pursue it in most matches because it opens a useful group route much more efficiently than hand tools; it is neither free at match start nor required to win. It should be among the costliest single things a team makes, yet affordable by one motivated player moving about two full 24-point material loads. The initial ingredient goal is in the catalog above and is a playtest placeholder, not a proven two-trip travel time; teammate and stockpile contributions can finish it sooner. At the carpenter, ram construction is a standing team project with an ingredient goal; no single player starts or owns it. Teammates contribute toward that goal from their own carried goods, and team stockpile goods may also be allocated to it. Record each reserved unit's source: the contributing player or the team stockpile. The ram is made when all required resources have been contributed; only then are reserved resources permanently spent. Its damage, movement, shared-spending controls, and counterplay are **open**; do not assume an extra-investment damage tier beyond the completion goal.
- **Ram refunds:** players cannot voluntarily cancel construction to use it as storage. If a game-defined event ends a ram project unfinished during the match, return stockpile-sourced units to the team stockpile. Return as many player-sourced units as fit by load in each original contributor's carried bag; keep their excess in an owner-only refund claim at the carpenter. Only that contributor can collect the remaining goods, in quantities that fit their current free load points. Pending claims cannot be used directly for crafting, team spending, or another contribution, and they never increase carried capacity. Each unit may be refunded or claimed only once. Unclaimed goods clear with the normal match reset. Which events can end an unfinished project is **open**.
- **Ram operator priority:** when the ram is completed, the teammate with the largest personal contribution to that ram gets the first chance to operate it. The first-playtest comparator is the total load points of direct personal donations, so different resources count in proportion to their bag load. Goods allocated from the pooled stockpile count for the team, not for whichever teammate triggered the allocation. If no personal contributor is available, another teammate may operate it; priority must not leave a completed ram unusable. The ram remains team equipment, not the contributor's property. Operator controls, ties, claim-window timing, and handoff are **open**. EXP is not required for this first playable rule.
- **Wall route:** destroying any wall section makes an entrance. Players can damage wall sections with hand tools, but this is deliberately inefficient. The battering ram can strike both gates and wall sections; its damage against each is **open**. Other dedicated siege tools may be designed later. A breach remains a gate door or wall section at 0 HP, even though a Keep can also be reached by tunnel.
- **Tunnel route:** one mirrored L-shaped, low-ceiling route per castle. The long leg begins at a concealed entrance outside the walls and runs toward the castle; the short leg turns into a forward part of the castle courtyard. It bypasses some open-field and gate fighting, but does not emerge beside the Keep or its stockpile room. The tunnel admits one traveler at a time, is crawl-only, slower than open-ground travel, and too narrow for a ram. Crawlers cannot fight while inside. Straight sections expose them to long-range fire and camping; the bend breaks a full-length sightline. Attackers must cross defended courtyard space and enter the Keep to reach the stockpile. A tunnel raider who reaches it may loot without a gate or wall breach. `layout.json` v2.1 contains the approved entrance, bend, exit, and interior coordinates; width, height, crawl speed, and travel time remain placeholders to validate with the controller and art.
- **Decided 2026-10-01:** park the planned movable siege cannon. A fixed castle ballista targets attacking players and siege tools, replacing the planned defensive cannon role. Whether a defender operates it, plus its placement, HP, rate of fire, ammunition, and cost are **open**. The old trebuchet and cannons remain parked. Ladders over the wall and personal vaults are later ideas.

## Legacy concepts — parked until redesigned for two teams

The following details describe the old free-for-all game. Their code and notes may remain for reference. They are not instructions to build these mechanics into the current siege. The v1 blacksmith uses a dedicated forge; other specialist stations and buildings await the village-like interior revision. Old rank, theft, negotiation, and villager systems stay parked.

### Old specialists

Old roles were blacksmith/armorer, alchemist, carpenter, farmer/miner, and merchants. The former theft, ranks, and rebellious flag assumed player-owned villages.
- Roles: **blacksmith/armorer** (battle weapons), **alchemist** (research), **carpenter** (siege equipment like trebuchets, plus castle building), **farmer/miner** NPCs (classic resource gathering). **Merchants**: randomly-occurring near castles, kept deliberately abstract for now.
- Specialists have investable skill rankings.
- Stealable: thief gets the specialist at a flat 85% efficiency (not scaled by skill level).
- Rebellious flag (player-set, hidden from raiders): **currently unresolved.** As designed, it has no real cost, meaning flagging everyone rebellious is strictly optimal — needs a fix before building. Two live options:
  - **(A) Standing efficiency tax:** flagged specialist works at reduced output at all times, whether stolen or not — simple, no RNG needed.
  - **(B) Standing defection risk:** flagged specialist keeps full efficiency, but carries an ongoing small chance of defecting/sabotaging even with no raid involved — this is the version that actually uses "probabilistic," but creates a passive background-monitoring feel similar to the decay mechanic that was already deliberately rejected elsewhere, so weigh that tension before picking it.
  - Pick one before building the rebellious flag.
- Spy variant (rebellious specialist, alternate outcome): reveals the base layout and the specific building they're held in, aiding the original owner's counter-raid.

### Old negotiation and reputation

Parked design. Written for player-vs-player theft in free-for-all; in a two-team match the negotiating parties are teams, which changes most of this.
- Theft of a specialist triggers negotiation between the two players, capped at a fixed number of rounds. The messenger cannot be refused outright (theft is deliberate, engagement is forced).
- Either side can escalate by killing the messenger — this enrages the killer's own troops and damages their reputation.
- Hitting the round cap with no resolution costs the thief, treated similarly to killing the messenger.
- **Important scope correction:** reputation is now within-match only. The original design had it persistent across sessions with recency-weighted decay — that assumed a persistent world, which no longer exists post-pivot. Reputation effects (mercenary availability, etc.) should reset with each new match.
- Bait (deliberately under-defending a location to lure a raid) is a real, later-stage feature — needs the base raid loop solid first.

## Cut or parked
- Persistent, no-wipe world.
- Cross-session/persistent faction reputation.
- Administrative distance decay from a faction capital.
- Player-vassals with real autonomy who can defect between factions.
- Resource node degradation as a check on compact monopolies.
- Free-for-all and last-squad-standing; player-built bases and villages; folding eliminated players into another team; AI troop regiments.
- Old trebuchet, defensive cannons, movable siege cannon, and tunnel collapse. Existing assets and code may remain dormant.
- No cross-match dominance; within-match snowball is untested — playtest.
