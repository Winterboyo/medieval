# Medieval Siege — game design (working v7, 2026-10-04)

This is the target game design for the two-team pivot. `AGENTS.md` holds working rules, the art pipeline, and tool instructions; `layout.json` v2.2 holds the approved exterior map and mirrored castle interior coordinates. `CASTLE-INTERIOR-V2-PROPOSAL.md` records the approved interior measurements and route reasoning. The repository shows implementation progress, not design authority. `CLAUDE.md` and `README.md` contain older descriptions and must not override this design. A change to this document does not silently change `layout.json` or the game code.

**Status words:** **Decided** means the user chose it. **Placeholder** is a number or simple behavior to test and tune. **Open** requires a user answer before dependent gameplay or art is built. **Parked** means preserve existing work but do not extend it. The open list at the end is part of the spec, not permission for an agent to invent answers.

## Core concept

Two-team medieval siege on one static island. Red and Blue defend mirrored, pre-built castles. Players gather resources, bring them home, improve their team's equipment and defenses, then assault the enemy vault inside the Keep. Combat is between players. A match is short and resets for the next queue. There are no AI troop regiments, player-built bases, persistent world, or cross-match resource losses. Destruction of the enemy vault wins; resource theft is not a match objective.

## Art direction (decided)
- **Decided 2026-10-03 (supersedes the two lines marked below):** the look moves toward *The Forge*'s textured, low-key style: low-poly shapes kept, every surface textured, Roblox Terrain for the ground, darker stone and wood, overcast daylight and firelit interiors (AGENTS.md §9, REALISM-TARGET.md). Map, layout and gameplay are unchanged.
- *(Superseded 2026-10-03, see above.)* Overall style: stylized low-poly — chunky, faceted geometry with flat angled surfaces, not smooth/rounded default Roblox shapes. Exaggerated proportions, saturated flat color separation between materials. Closer to a fantasy-medieval low-poly asset-pack look than realistic or default-Studio terrain.
- Resource node identity follows Catan's approach: ground material can stay simple and consistent — a node's identity comes from a distinct built prop scene sitting on top of it, not from ground color alone:
  - **Wood** — a cluster of low-poly trees with chunky faceted round canopies. These are the ONLY round-canopy trees on the map; decorative trees are stacked-cone pines.
  - **Stone** — a jagged angular rock outcropping, distinctly lighter/grayer than Ore so the two read as different at a glance.
  - **Ore** — a mine entrance or exposed vein: dark rock with small embedded glinting chunks.
  - **Grain (parked)** — a golden wheat-stalk cluster or a strongly colored field patch if a later recipe justifies adding it. It is not a first-playable resource node.
  - **Wool** — a small fenced pasture with a few sheep models scattered in it.
- Castle: use the reference family and asset contract in `AGENTS.md` §§9–12. Round towers are faceted cylinders and cones, scaled to a 5-stud avatar. The current castle kit is a prototype; new Keep damage states, tunnel pieces, and any weapon change need their own approved asset tasks.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — recipe-to-model rule:** crafted equipment visibly uses the materials in its recipe. For example, the crossbow shows a wooden stock, iron mechanism, and wool component; the ram shows its timber frame and log, stone ballast, wool canopy, and iron striking head/brackets. Show each ingredient clearly without making the silhouette hard to read. The Stone-and-Wool bow needs a reviewed visual construction before its asset is built.
- **Decided 2026-10-06:** inside the castle walls, use a compact village-like arrangement of distinct small working buildings around paths and a courtyard. The Minecraft-village comparison describes spatial feel, not copied art or trading mechanics. The forge replaces the shared workshop. `layout.json` v2.2 contains approved mirrored positions for the Keep and its former stockpile room, barracks, four specialists, ram pad, tower access, and ballista mount; `CASTLE-INTERIOR-V2-PROPOSAL.md` explains measurements. The former stockpile room becomes the vault room. New interior art still needs Blender review.
- *(Superseded 2026-10-03: ground moves to Roblox Terrain.)* **Ground terrain is part of the style, not just props.** Build it in Blender from `layout.json` and import static FBX tiles: a green island, faceted shoreline, and sea outside the playable area. Avoid Roblox SmoothTerrain for this look.

- Shallow craters and small barricades are permanent battlefield cover. Players can crouch behind crater rims and barricades; each must provide actual sightline protection at avatar scale. Place them in mirrored pairs beside the road, with clear routes around them. Their exact footprints, heights, and positions belong in the revised layout proposal.

## Match contract

- **Decided 2026-10-06:** two fixed teams, Red and Blue, targeting 15v15. There is no player elimination or friendly fire. Players respawn on their own side after being knocked down. The pre-built castles and map reset between matches. For the first playtest, queue for about 45 seconds, then start with equal teams of at least 3v3; 2v2 is for private mechanical tests only. Auto-assign new players to the smaller team, allow backfill until overtime, never force-switch an active player, and keep parties together in the queue. If one team has no players for 30 seconds, it forfeits. These queue values are test placeholders, and 3v3 does not validate 15v15 balance. `layout.json` v2.2 has only six barracks spawn positions per side, so a separate measured layout proposal and approval are required before adding enough spawn positions for 15v15.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** terrain shapes and castle positions remain mirrored across `layout.json`'s x = 0 axis. The map stays static. Each side must have equal counts and types of active resource nodes, but the positions of corresponding nodes may differ within equivalent approved regions. This replaces the earlier exact mirrored-node-slot requirement for future procedural node placement; `layout.json` v2.2 still owns current slots, and a layout proposal/approval is required before changing those coordinates or generated art.
- **Decided 2026-10-06:** each vault inside its Keep has 750 HP for the first playtest. A team wins immediately when the enemy vault reaches 0 HP. Attackers may damage it whenever they physically reach it; neither a gate nor a wall breach is required. Hits through a closed wall, door, or floor do not count. The Keep exterior has no damage bar during regulation; the overtime rule below activates a separate Keep exterior HP target. A win ends the match and returns players to the lobby for the next match.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — no preparation phase (supersedes the 2026-10-01 choice):** when the 15-second lobby/reset countdown ends and the match clock starts, both teams can move across the full playable map, fight, damage structures, gather, carry, craft, deposit, repair, and contribute to the ram immediately. The approximate 45-second matchmaking queue occurs before that lobby countdown and is outside the match clock. Home, forward, and shared-center resource areas are available from match start. There is no protected opening, temporary side boundary, or later combat-unlock transition.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — starting and respawn equipment:** every player starts with free basic Axe, Pickaxe, and Shears, and has those tools restored without duplicates after knockdown. First-playtest basic-tool damage is 12 per hit, below the crafted Wood Sword. Players start with no sword, armor, bow, crossbow, ammunition, or carried materials. The Wood Sword is the lowest-tier crafted combat weapon. Completed personal gear and items, including crafted weapons, armor, unused ammunition, and bandages, remain with their owner through knockdown; only carried crafting materials enter the fallen cargo bag. Gear resets with the match, not with each knockdown.
- **First-playtest match clock (accepted 2026-10-06):** regulation lasts 13 minutes. If neither vault has fallen, a 2-minute overtime begins; both Keep exteriors gain a fresh, separate 900 HP target while their vaults retain their remaining HP. During overtime, destroying the enemy vault or Keep exterior wins immediately. All shared structure repair is disabled in overtime. At the 15-minute hard cap, compare remaining vault HP, then remaining Keep exterior HP; an exact tie is a draw. These durations and HP values are test placeholders. The intended pacing is an armed fight within 90 seconds, first ram around 3–5 minutes, and first serious vault attack around 5–8 minutes; most matches should end before overtime. Observe a roughly 60/40 fighting-to-gathering activity mix rather than enforcing it. `layout.json` has no shrinking zone; the old free-for-all zone is parked.

## Resources and team economy

- **Decided 2026-10-06 — first-playable gathered resources:** Wood, Stone, Ore, and Wool. Grain is parked until a useful recipe justifies it. Smelted Iron is crafted from Ore at the Blacksmith, not a field node. Raw materials and Smelted Iron can be carried or deposited into the team vault. Finished personal gear remains with the player through knockdown. Gear handoff is deferred for the first playable. The approved `layout.json` v2.2 still allows Grain in some slots; removing it from allowed node types requires an approved layout revision.
- **Decided 2026-10-06:** gathering and foraging remain meaningful throughout the match, including while players fight and siege. Keep reachable home, middle, forward, and shared resource areas off the ram routes, all available from match start. Start with the existing 16 node slots and static terrain. For each match, vary active resource types and positions only among approved safe pads in equivalent regions; each team gets equal counts of each type, but corresponding positions need not mirror exactly. Do not place Ore only in the contested center; its base gathering time matches other resources. Nodes have finite supply, deplete when gathered, and regenerate on a curve fastest at moderate depletion. Recipe costs, smelting, travel, and availability pace high-tier gear. The current slot count and distribution require a 15v15 playtest, and changed pads or coordinates require a measured layout proposal and approval before art or generation changes. Exact gather time and regeneration rates remain test placeholders.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** gathered goods go into the gathering player's weighted bag. The player chooses to carry ingredients directly to a specialist or voluntarily deposit them in the team vault. Depositing is not a prerequisite for personal crafting. Carried goods form a ransackable bag immediately at the player's knockdown location (fallen cargo, below).
- **First-playtest material bag (accepted 2026-10-06 placeholders):** one mixed bag uses weighted load points: Wood and Wool cost 1 each, Stone 2, Ore and Smelted Iron 4 each. Up to 20 points carries at normal speed; above 20 through 25 points applies a 20% movement-speed penalty, including while sprinting. No pickup may exceed 25. Gathering depletes a node only by accepted whole units. Smelting 1 Ore into 1 Iron preserves load. Tune the penalty after playtest; whether a player may refuse an overloading pickup remains open.
- **Decided 2026-10-06 — visible carried bag:** each player wears their bag on their back while it holds anything, and it shows the load: the sack body swells with total load, the mouth is heaped with the good carrying the most load, and a smaller accent shows the second. An empty bag is not shown. Asset and piece names are in `AGENTS.md` §11 ("Fallen bag"); the same model will serve the fallen cargo bag.
- **Decided 2026-10-04 — equipment choice target:** an Iron sword, a strong set of individually crafted armor pieces, and a Crossbow with usable bolts compete for time and materials. A typical player should choose which to pursue first during the live match; this is a pacing target, not a hard equipment lock. Wood and Stone gear are options, not prerequisites for Iron gear. Bag loads limit each trip but not total crafting. Recipe quantities, travel, and forge time must be tuned together; the first-playtest recipe table below remains provisional.
- **Gather → carry → craft or deposit loop:** gathering moves whole raw units from a finite node into that player's weighted bag. Personal items use only that player's carried ingredients, never team deposits. At the vault, **Deposit all** and **Choose goods and amounts** let a player contribute some or all carried resources to the shared team balance; every transfer subtracts exactly what it credits. Deposits cannot be withdrawn for personal crafting. Shared gate/wall/tower repairs and defenses spend the team balance through their own interactions. The ram accepts source-tracked carried or vault contributions. Enemy players cannot withdraw, loot, or ransack the vault. Spending permissions, deposit timing, and output delivery remain **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-01 — fallen cargo, clarified 2026-10-03, reconfirmed 2026-10-05:** at knockdown, transfer only the player's carried crafting materials exactly once into a bag at that position and clear their carried material cargo. Completed personal items remain with the player. The 30-second lifetime starts immediately; the later respawn does not create another bag or restart the timer. The bag stays there; nobody picks up or carries the bag object. Any player on either team may ransack its contents. Enemy players steal resources into their own carried bag; teammates recover them into theirs. Ransacked goods stay carried until that player crafts with or deposits them; they never credit a team automatically. The bag disappears when emptied or 30 seconds after knockdown, whichever comes first. Ransacking never refreshes its timer; resources left at expiry leave the match without crediting either team. (The 2026-10-04 revision wrongly marked this rule removed; `AGENTS.md` kept it.)
- **Decided 2026-10-01 — bag ransacking, revised for weighted load 2026-10-06:** within 6 studs of a fallen bag, if the total load of every resource remaining in it fits in the player's free load points (up to the 25-point hard cap), offer **Take all**; one 2-second stationary hold transfers the entire contents. If it does not all fit, show the bag's resource types and quantities; the player selects which goods and amounts to take, up to their free load points, then completes one 2-second stationary hold. Do not automatically fill remaining load with unselected goods. Moving, taking damage, leaving range, or bag expiry cancels the hold with no transfer. On completion, recheck bag contents and free load, transfer only selected goods still available and fitting, subtract exactly what was granted, and leave the rest in the bag. A player without enough free load for any remaining resource cannot ransack it. Concurrent completed holds cannot duplicate goods. Ore and Smelted Iron can both be stolen this way. Range and hold time are first-playtest placeholders.
- **Decided:** resources earned in-match pay for repairs, gear crafting, and siege equipment. Personal items use the crafter's carried ingredients. The ram can use carried and vault contributions; shared repairs and ballista construction use vault deposits. Coins cannot purchase combat power. Bow and crossbow are the ranged hand weapons. Exact costs remain placeholders or open as stated in the catalog.
- **Superseded 2026-10-04:** the earlier one-time 20% stockpile snapshot and later logarithmic looting model are both removed. The enemy cannot steal from the vault. The match objective is physical destruction of the enemy vault. Vault theft is gone; fallen-bag ransacking (above) remains.

### First-playable crafting catalog (chosen 2026-10-03)

These are chosen outputs and material types; numeric quantities are first-playtest placeholders unless explicitly decided. Personal items spend carried ingredients only. Smelting consumes exactly 1 carried Ore and produces exactly 1 carried Smelted Iron; both weigh 4 load points. Iron may be carried to another specialist, contributed to construction, or deposited in the team vault. Neither Ore nor Iron can be stolen from the vault; both can be ransacked from a fallen bag.

- **Blacksmith/forge:** smelt Ore into Iron. Craft Wood, Stone, and Iron swords and separately craft armor for head, chest, and legs at Wood, Stone, or Iron tier. Each sword and armor piece may be made directly from carried ingredients at its full standalone cost: no lower tier item or discount is required. On success, a new sword replaces the old sword; a new armor piece replaces only the piece in that same body slot. On failure or interruption, existing gear and ingredients remain unchanged. First-playtest sword damage is 24/32/40 for Wood/Stone/Iron. Armor reduces incoming damage by the percentages in the combat section. An equip animation that slides pieces over the head, chest, and legs is wanted later, not a first-playtest gate. Iron gear uses Smelted Iron, and Wool is visible padding in armor.
- **Fletcher:** craft a Bow from Wool and Stone, Bow Arrows from Wood and Stone, a Crossbow from Wood, Wool, and Smelted Iron, Crossbow Bolts, and larger Ballista Bolts requiring Smelted Iron. A successfully crafted Crossbow removes the player's Bow, if held, but the Bow is neither an ingredient nor a discount; failure leaves it intact. First-playtest Bow and Crossbow hit damage is 28 and 49 (1.75×); the Crossbow reload takes about 2.5 seconds. Carry limits are 24 Bow Arrows and 16 Crossbow Bolts. Bow draw/cadence and the Stone-and-Wool Bow's visible construction remain open. The Bolt Pouch is a later idea in `IDEAS.md`.
- **Alchemist:** craft Wool-based Bandages. Each Bandage restores approximately 15% of maximum HP (placeholder), takes a 2-second use, and a player may carry at most three. Taking damage interrupts use without consuming the Bandage. Cooldown remains open.
- **Carpenter/team projects:** the Battering Ram placeholder recipe is 18 Wood, 8 Stone, 4 Wool, and 2 Smelted Iron: 32 material units or 46 weighted load points. It can be brought in two overloaded 25-point trips by a motivated solo player, though gathering and travel may require more trips. Wood forms frame and log, Stone ballast, Wool canopy, and Iron striking head/brackets. The ram accepts shared contributions and refunds as below. A fixed defensive Ballista is also built by team construction, uses Fletcher-made bolts, and is funded from the vault. First-playtest ballista and shared repair costs are listed below.

## Castle and assault

- **Decided 2026-10-06:** each castle has one wall ring with identified sections, four corner towers, one gate with a separate door, a vault room inside the Keep, and team-colored banners. `layout.json` v2.2 owns the approved geometry: 183.6 × 183.6 ring, 35 wall sections, castle centers at x = ±420, inward-facing gates at x = ±328.2, and a 44 × 44 Keep with an 18 × 12 former stockpile room to serve as the vault room. The vault is a separate physical target from the Keep exterior. The Keep entrance is traversable by both teams once reached; the castle gate controls the main outer-wall route. Existing geometry remains unchanged.
- **Decided 2026-10-06:** defenders can stand on tower tops via stairs or ladders. Gate, wall sections, towers, and the vault have independent HP and intact/damaged/rubble visuals. First-playtest values are gate door 600 HP, each wall section 300 HP, each tower 500 HP, and vault 750 HP. The vault cannot be repaired. The Keep exterior has no HP during regulation; both exteriors become 900 HP targets at overtime start. Players can damage the vault with hand tools, swords, and arrows only when the attack actually reaches it. The ram damages only the outer gate. First-playtest shared repair spends 2 Wood + 2 Stone from the vault to restore 100 HP to a standing gate, wall section, tower, or ballista after a 3-second hold, provided that piece has not taken damage for 8 seconds. It cannot restore a destroyed piece, exceed maximum HP, or occur in overtime. Weapon damage multipliers against structures and damage-state thresholds remain open.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** the first playable has a barracks and four crafting specialists in separate village buildings: blacksmith, fletcher, carpenter, and alchemist. The blacksmith's forge replaces the shared workshop and smelts Ore; the Fletcher makes Bow, Crossbow, and ammunition; the Carpenter handles ram and other team construction; the Alchemist makes Bandages. NPCs craft only: no ranks, theft, negotiation, or trading. The barracks houses team spawns and a protected exit. The former stockpile room inside the Keep becomes the vault room, which accepts team deposits and is the physical win target. The approved room coordinates are in `layout.json` v2.2. Mason, cook, and weaver remain in `IDEAS.md`.
- **Gate route, revised 2026-10-06:** teammates and their own completed ram pass through their own intact outer gate; enemies and enemy rams collide with it until they damage it to 0 HP. This is team-specific passage, with no manual open control for a defender to misuse. Heavy player hits or a ram can destroy the gate. Show the friendly passage clearly in play so it does not look like accidental clipping. The ram is optional but should be pursued in most matches; target first completion around minute 4. One active ram per team is the first-playtest limit; a destroyed ram may be rebuilt. It is a standing team project at the Carpenter: teammates contribute from carried goods or authorize vault-funded contributions, each source tracked for refunds. The ram appears once its full ingredient goal is met. After completion, multiple teammates may join or leave its moving/operating crew without losing contribution credit. The largest personal contributor gets a 5-second first chance to operate it; if unavailable, any teammate may take over. Up to three teammates can push it, at placeholder speeds of 6/8/9 studs per second for one/two/three pushers. First-playtest ram HP is 700; an operated ram deals 120 damage to the gate every 3 seconds. Crew controls and exact collision/animation remain to be validated.
- **Ram refunds:** players cannot cancel unfinished construction to use it as storage. If a game-defined event ends a project unfinished, return vault-sourced units to the vault. Return player-sourced units to each original contributor's bag up to its free weighted load capacity; place excess in an owner-only claim at the Carpenter. Only that contributor can collect what fits later. Claims cannot be spent or collected by teammates and clear at match reset. Which events close a project and whether overload capacity may be used for automatic refunds are **open**.
- **Ram operator priority:** the teammate with the largest direct personal contribution gets the first 5 seconds to operate a completed ram. Compare weighted load points donated directly; vault allocation grants no individual priority. After the window, any teammate may operate. A contributor who disconnects or leaves the ram crew cannot block use, and contribution credit persists for the match. The ram remains team equipment; up to three teammates can push at once. Equal-contribution ties need a deterministic rule before implementation. EXP rewards can follow later.
- **Wall route, revised 2026-10-04:** destroying any wall section makes an entrance. Hand tools and other player weapons may damage walls, but doing so is deliberately inefficient. The battering ram exclusively damages the gate; it cannot strike wall sections. Other dedicated wall-breaching siege tools are planned for later design. A breach remains a gate door or wall section at 0 HP, even though the vault can also be reached by tunnel.
- **Tunnel route, revised 2026-10-04:** one mirrored L-shaped low-ceiling route per castle has a concealed outer entrance, long crawl leg, bend, and an exit in the forward courtyard away from the vault. It skips part of the open-field and gate fight, but attackers must cross defended courtyard space to reach the vault. It admits one crawler at a time, moves slower than open-ground travel, and cannot carry a ram. Crawlers cannot fight while inside. A defender at either entrance/exit can shoot down a straight section with a crossbow, while the bend breaks a full-length sightline; firing from inside the tunnel is not required. `layout.json` v2.2 owns current coordinates. Width, crawl speed, and travel time remain placeholders for avatar-scale validation.
- **Ballista, revised 2026-10-06:** one fixed castle ballista per team may be built once per match through vault-funded shared construction (8 Wood + 4 Stone + 2 Wool + 2 Smelted Iron). It has 400 HP for the first playtest and a forward firing arc that must be checked against the approved mount position. A defender operates it manually; it holds up to eight Fletcher-made Ballista Bolts, consumes one per shot, and reloads in 5 seconds. First-playtest damage is 70 to a player or 140 to a ram. Enemy attacks and siege damage can damage it; any teammate may authorize construction or repair from the vault, but repair while it is under attack is disallowed. Use the shared structure repair rule above while it stands; destruction leaves it unavailable for the rest of the match. Precise firing arc and bolt loading interaction need in-game validation. The old trebuchet and cannons remain parked.

## Combat, movement, and onboarding (revised 2026-10-04)

- **Controls (desktop first playable):** WASD moves; Shift sprints; Ctrl crouches; F blocks; left click swings or fires the equipped ranged weapon; right click aims; R reloads a Crossbow; E handles nearby contextual interactions; G opens or closes the player's carried-material bag; M opens the full map. Each Crossbow shot consumes one carried Crossbow Bolt. Mobile/gamepad equivalents still need explicit mappings. Crouching must work behind crater rims and barricades at normal gameplay camera height.
- **First-playtest combat values (accepted 2026-10-06, tune after playtest):** base player health 100. Basic tools deal 12 damage; Wood/Stone/Iron swords deal 24/32/40; Bow and Crossbow hits deal 28/49, before armor. Crossbow reload is about 2.5 seconds. Armor mitigation is additive across equipped pieces and capped at 60%: Wood head/chest/legs provide 4/8/6%, Stone 8/16/12%, Iron 12/24/18%. Damage taken is raw damage multiplied by (1 − total mitigation); there is no armor durability. F blocks frontal melee for 50% less damage while draining stamina, but does not block arrows; armor still applies to an arrow hit. Sprint, crouch, and block belong in the first playable. Exact stamina costs/regen, attack cadence, Bow draw, hit feedback, and animation timings remain open. The armor equip animation and any dodge move may follow after the first playable.
- **Barracks respawn (first-playtest placeholder):** enemies cannot enter the barracks spawn room. A respawning player is protected for up to 4 seconds, including no more than 2 seconds after leaving the protected doorway; attacking ends protection immediately, and a protected player cannot deal damage outward. This prevents immediate spawn camping without extending immunity into the fight. The spawn-room boundary and 15v15 spawn positions need measured layout validation.
- **First playable scope:** four crafting specialists, Wood/Stone/Ore/Wool nodes, weighted carried bags and fallen-bag ransacking, personal crafting, team vault deposits and damage, directly crafted swords and armor pieces, Bow/Crossbow with ammunition, Bandages, a gate-only ram, fixed defensive ballista, tunnel, sprint/crouch/block, and essential HUD prompts. The introductory tutorial, dodge, armor equip animation, other wall siege tools, EXP progression, and cosmetics follow later.
- **Onboarding:** the first playtest should communicate gathering, crafting, contribution, defending, and vault assault through visible world cues and contextual prompts. An introductory tutorial comes after observing real players' stuck points.

## Legacy concepts — parked until redesigned for two teams

The following details describe the old free-for-all game. Their code and notes may remain for reference. They are not instructions to build these mechanics into the current siege. The v1 blacksmith uses a dedicated forge; other specialist stations and buildings await the village-like interior revision. Old rank, theft, negotiation, and villager systems stay parked.

### Old specialists

Old roles were blacksmith/armorer, alchemist, carpenter, farmer/miner, and merchants. The former theft, ranks, and rebellious flag assumed player-owned villages.
- Roles: **blacksmith/armorer** (battle weapons), **alchemist** (research), **carpenter** (siege equipment like trebuchets, plus castle building), **farmer/miner** NPCs (classic resource gathering). **Merchants**: randomly-occurring near castles, kept deliberately abstract for now.
- Specialists have investable skill rankings.
- Stealable: thief gets the specialist at a flat 85% efficiency (not scaled by skill level).
- Rebellious flag (player-set, hidden from raiders): **currently unresolved.** As designed, it has no real cost, meaning flagging everyone rebellious is strictly optimal — needs a fix before building. Two live options:
  - **(A) Standing efficiency tax:** flagged specialist works at reduced output at all times, whether stolen or not — simple, no RNG needed.
  - **(B) Standing defection risk:** flagged specialist keeps full efficiency, but carries an ongoing small chance of defecting/sabotaging even with no raid involved — this is the version that actually uses "probabilistic," but creates a passive background-monitoring feel similar to the decay mechanic that was already deliberately rejected elsewhere, so weigh that tension before picking it.
  - Pick one before building the rebellious flag.
- Spy variant (rebellious specialist, alternate outcome): reveals the base layout and the specific building they're held in, aiding the original owner's counter-raid.

### Old negotiation and reputation

Parked design. Written for player-vs-player theft in free-for-all; in a two-team match the negotiating parties are teams, which changes most of this.
- Theft of a specialist triggers negotiation between the two players, capped at a fixed number of rounds. The messenger cannot be refused outright (theft is deliberate, engagement is forced).
- Either side can escalate by killing the messenger — this enrages the killer's own troops and damages their reputation.
- Hitting the round cap with no resolution costs the thief, treated similarly to killing the messenger.
- **Important scope correction:** reputation is now within-match only. The original design had it persistent across sessions with recency-weighted decay — that assumed a persistent world, which no longer exists post-pivot. Reputation effects (mercenary availability, etc.) should reset with each new match.
- Bait (deliberately under-defending a location to lure a raid) is a real, later-stage feature — needs the base raid loop solid first.
