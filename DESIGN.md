# Medieval Siege — game design (working v7, 2026-10-04)

This is the target game design for the two-team pivot. `AGENTS.md` holds working rules, the art pipeline, and tool instructions; `layout.json` v2.2 holds the approved exterior map and mirrored castle interior coordinates. `CASTLE-INTERIOR-V2-PROPOSAL.md` records the approved interior measurements and route reasoning. The repository shows implementation progress, not design authority. `CLAUDE.md` and `README.md` contain older descriptions and must not override this design. A change to this document does not silently change `layout.json` or the game code.

**Status words:** **Decided** means the user chose it. **Placeholder** is a number or simple behavior to test and tune. **Open** requires a user answer before dependent gameplay or art is built. **Parked** means preserve existing work but do not extend it. The open list at the end is part of the spec, not permission for an agent to invent answers.

## Core concept

Two-team medieval siege on one static island. Red and Blue defend mirrored, pre-built castles. Players gather resources, bring them home, improve their team's equipment and defenses, then assault the enemy vault inside the Keep. Combat is between players. A match is short and resets for the next queue. There are no AI troop regiments, player-built bases, persistent world, or cross-match resource losses. Destruction of the enemy vault wins; resource theft is not a match objective.

## Art direction (decided)
- **Decided 2026-10-03 (supersedes the two lines marked below):** the look moves toward *The Forge*'s textured, low-key style: low-poly shapes kept, every surface textured, Roblox Terrain for the ground, darker stone and wood, overcast daylight and firelit interiors (AGENTS.md §9, REALISM-TARGET.md). Map, layout and gameplay are unchanged.
- *(Superseded 2026-10-03, see above.)* Overall style: stylized low-poly — chunky, faceted geometry with flat angled surfaces, not smooth/rounded default Roblox shapes. Exaggerated proportions, saturated flat color separation between materials. Closer to a fantasy-medieval low-poly asset-pack look than realistic or default-Studio terrain.
- Resource node identity follows Catan's approach: ground material can stay simple and consistent — a node's identity comes from a distinct built prop scene sitting on top of it, not from ground color alone:
  - **Wood** — a cluster of low-poly trees with chunky faceted round canopies. These are the ONLY round-canopy trees on the map; decorative trees are stacked-cone pines.
  - **Stone** — a jagged angular rock outcropping, distinctly lighter/grayer than Ore so the two read as different at a glance.
  - **Ore** — a mine entrance or exposed vein: dark rock with small embedded glinting chunks.
  - **Grain (parked)** — a golden wheat-stalk cluster or a strongly colored field patch if a later recipe justifies adding it. It is not a first-playable resource node.
  - **Wool** — a small fenced pasture with a few sheep models scattered in it.
- Castle: use the reference family and asset contract in `AGENTS.md` §§9–12. Round towers are faceted cylinders and cones, scaled to a 5-stud avatar. The current castle kit is a prototype; new Keep damage states, tunnel pieces, and any weapon change need their own approved asset tasks.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — recipe-to-model rule:** crafted equipment visibly uses the materials in its recipe. For example, the crossbow shows a wooden stock, iron mechanism, and wool component; the ram shows its timber frame and log, stone ballast, wool canopy, and iron striking head/brackets. Show each ingredient clearly without making the silhouette hard to read. The Stone-and-Wool bow needs a reviewed visual construction before its asset is built.
- **Decided 2026-10-01:** inside the castle walls, use a compact village-like arrangement of distinct small working buildings around paths and a courtyard. The Minecraft-village comparison describes spatial feel, not copied art or trading mechanics. The forge replaces the shared workshop. `layout.json` v2.2 contains approved mirrored positions for the Keep and its former stockpile room, barracks, four specialists, ram pad, tower access, and ballista mount; `CASTLE-INTERIOR-V2-PROPOSAL.md` explains measurements. The former stockpile room becomes the vault room, subject to the user's answer on the vault's damage model. New interior art still needs Blender review.
- *(Superseded 2026-10-03: ground moves to Roblox Terrain.)* **Ground terrain is part of the style, not just props.** Build it in Blender from `layout.json` and import static FBX tiles: a green island, faceted shoreline, and sea outside the playable area. Avoid Roblox SmoothTerrain for this look.

## Match contract

- **Decided 2026-10-04:** two fixed teams, Red and Blue, targeting 6v6. There is no player elimination or friendly fire. Players respawn on their own side after being knocked down. The pre-built castles and map reset between matches. Rules for starting with fewer players, uneven teams, and players leaving mid-match are **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** terrain shapes and castle positions remain mirrored across `layout.json`'s x = 0 axis. The map stays static. Each side must have equal counts and types of active resource nodes, but the positions of corresponding nodes may differ within equivalent approved regions. This replaces the earlier exact mirrored-node-slot requirement for future procedural node placement; `layout.json` v2.2 still owns current slots, and a layout proposal/approval is required before changing those coordinates or generated art.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** a team wins when the enemy vault inside the Keep reaches 0 HP. Attackers may target it whenever they reach it; a gate or wall breach is not a prerequisite. The vault's relationship to the Keep damage model is being clarified below. The win ends the match and returns players to the lobby for the next match.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — no preparation phase (supersedes the 2026-10-01 choice):** when the lobby ends and the match starts, both teams can move across the full playable map, fight, damage structures, gather, carry, craft, deposit, repair, and contribute to the ram immediately. Home, forward, and shared-center resource areas are available from match start. There is no protected opening, temporary side boundary, or later combat-unlock transition. The 15-second lobby is a queue/reset interval, not a gathering phase.
- **Decided 2026-10-03 — starting and respawn equipment:** every player starts with free basic Axe, Pickaxe, and Shears, and has those tools restored without duplicates after knockdown. These tools can deal weak melee damage so an uncrafted player can defend themself; their exact damage is **open** but must stay below the crafted Wood Sword's combat value. Players start with no sword, armor, bow, crossbow, ammunition, or carried materials. The Wood Sword is the lowest-tier crafted combat weapon. Completed personal gear and items, including crafted weapons, armor, unused ammunition, and bandages, remain with their owner through knockdown; only carried crafting materials enter the fallen cargo bag. Gear resets with the match, not with each knockdown.
- **Placeholder 2026-10-04:** keep the current 13-minute hard cap for testing, subject to playtest rather than an asserted retention optimum. At timeout, the team with more remaining vault HP wins. The exact tie result is **open**. The intended pacing is an immediate possible armed rush once a player crafts a weapon, first ram around 4 minutes, first serious vault attack around 6.5 minutes, and most matches ending before the cap. Close matches may reach the cap. Observe a roughly 60/40 fighting-to-gathering activity mix rather than enforcing it.
- **Open:** minimum/uneven-team matchmaking, party behavior, and departures during a match. `layout.json` has no shrinking zone. The old free-for-all zone is parked unless the user explicitly restores it.

## Resources and team economy

- **Decided 2026-10-04 — first-playable gathered resources:** Wood, Stone, Ore, and Wool. Grain is parked until a useful recipe justifies it. Smelted Iron is crafted from Ore at the Blacksmith, not a field node. The approved `layout.json` v2.2 still allows Grain in some resource slots; changing their types or positions requires a new approved layout revision. Raw materials and Smelted Iron can be carried or deposited into the team vault. Finished personal gear remains with the player through knockdown. Voluntary gear handoff is **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** gathering and foraging remain meaningful throughout the match, including while players fight and siege. The map must retain reachable home, middle, forward, and shared resource areas off the ram routes; all are accessible from match start. Nodes have finite supply, deplete when gathered, and regenerate on a curve fastest at moderate depletion. Each team gets equal counts and types, though future approved node positions need not mirror exactly. Ore should be no more contested than other resources and should take the same base gathering time; high-tier gear pacing should come from recipe costs, smelting, travel, and availability instead. Exact node counts, region assignments, and gather time are **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** gathered goods go into the gathering player's weighted bag. The player chooses to carry ingredients directly to a specialist or voluntarily deposit them in the team vault. Depositing is not a prerequisite for personal crafting. Carried goods form a ransackable bag immediately at the player's knockdown location (fallen cargo, below).
- **First-playtest material bag (revised 2026-10-04 placeholders):** one mixed bag uses weighted load points: Wood and Wool cost 1 each, Stone 2, Ore and Smelted Iron 4 each. Up to 20 points carries at normal speed; whole units may bring load to 25 points, but the player moves slower while above 20. No pickup may exceed 25. Gathering depletes a node only by accepted whole units. Smelting 1 Ore into 1 Iron preserves load. Exact slowdown, interaction with sprint, and whether a player may opt to refuse an overloading pickup are **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-04 — equipment choice target:** an Iron sword, a strong set of individually crafted armor pieces, and a Crossbow with usable bolts compete for time and materials. A typical player should choose which to pursue first during the live match; this is a pacing target, not a hard equipment lock. Wood and Stone gear are options, not prerequisites for Iron gear. Bag loads limit each trip but not total crafting. Recipe quantities, travel, and forge time must be tuned together; the first-playtest recipe table below remains provisional.
- **Gather → carry → craft or deposit loop:** gathering moves whole raw units from a finite node into that player's weighted bag. Personal items use only that player's carried ingredients, never team deposits. At the vault, **Deposit all** and **Choose goods and amounts** let a player contribute some or all carried resources to the shared team balance; every transfer subtracts exactly what it credits. Deposits cannot be withdrawn for personal crafting. Shared gate/wall/tower repairs and defenses spend the team balance through their own interactions. The ram accepts source-tracked carried or vault contributions. Enemy players cannot withdraw, loot, or ransack the vault. Spending permissions, deposit timing, and output delivery remain **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-01 — fallen cargo, clarified 2026-10-03, reconfirmed 2026-10-05:** at knockdown, transfer only the player's carried crafting materials exactly once into a bag at that position and clear their carried material cargo. Completed personal items remain with the player. The 30-second lifetime starts immediately; the later respawn does not create another bag or restart the timer. The bag stays there; nobody picks up or carries the bag object. Any player on either team may ransack its contents. Enemy players steal resources into their own carried bag; teammates recover them into theirs. Ransacked goods stay carried until that player crafts with or deposits them; they never credit a team automatically. The bag disappears when emptied or 30 seconds after knockdown, whichever comes first. Ransacking never refreshes its timer; resources left at expiry leave the match without crediting either team. (The 2026-10-04 revision wrongly marked this rule removed; `AGENTS.md` kept it.)
- **Decided 2026-10-01 — bag ransacking, revised for weighted load 2026-10-03:** if the total load of every resource remaining in the fallen bag fits in the player's free load points (up to the 25-point hard cap), offer **Take all**; one 2-second stationary hold transfers the entire contents. If it does not all fit, show the bag's resource types and quantities; the player selects which goods and amounts to take, up to their free load points, then completes one 2-second stationary hold. Do not automatically fill remaining load with unselected goods. Moving, taking damage, or bag expiry cancels the hold with no transfer. On completion, recheck bag contents and free load, transfer only selected goods still available and fitting, subtract exactly what was granted, and leave the rest in the bag. A player without enough free load for any remaining resource cannot ransack it. Concurrent completed holds cannot duplicate goods. Ore and Smelted Iron can both be stolen this way. The 2-second hold is tunable after playtest; interaction range is **open**.
- **Decided:** resources earned in-match pay for repairs, gear crafting, and siege equipment. Personal items use the crafter's carried ingredients. The ram can use carried and vault contributions; shared repairs and ballista construction use vault deposits. Coins cannot purchase combat power. Bow and crossbow are the ranged hand weapons. Exact costs remain placeholders or open as stated in the catalog.
- **Superseded 2026-10-04:** the earlier one-time 20% stockpile snapshot and later logarithmic looting model are both removed. The enemy cannot steal from the vault. The match objective is physical destruction of the enemy vault. Vault theft is gone; fallen-bag ransacking (above) remains.

### First-playable crafting catalog (chosen 2026-10-03)

These are chosen outputs and material types; numeric quantities are first-playtest placeholders unless explicitly decided. Personal items spend carried ingredients only. Smelting consumes exactly 1 carried Ore and produces exactly 1 carried Smelted Iron; both weigh 4 load points. Iron may be carried to another specialist, contributed to construction, or deposited in the team vault. Neither Ore nor Iron can be stolen from the vault; both can be ransacked from a fallen bag.

- **Blacksmith/forge:** smelt Ore into Iron. Craft Wood, Stone, and Iron swords and separately craft armor for head, chest, and legs at Wood, Stone, or Iron tier. Each sword and each armor piece may be made directly from carried ingredients at its full standalone cost: no lower tier item or discount is required. On success, a new sword replaces the old sword; a new armor piece replaces only the piece in that same body slot. On failure or interruption, existing gear and ingredients remain unchanged. Armor reduces incoming damage by a percentage; the mitigation percentages and how multiple pieces combine are **open**. Sword tiers differ by damage only; exact damage is **open**. An equip animation that slides pieces over the head, chest, and legs is wanted later, not a first-playtest gate. Iron gear uses Smelted Iron, and Wool is visible padding in armor.
- **Fletcher:** craft a Bow from Wool and Stone, Bow Arrows from Wood and Stone, a Crossbow from Wood, Wool, and Smelted Iron, Crossbow Bolts, and larger Ballista Bolts requiring Smelted Iron. A successfully crafted Crossbow removes the player's Bow, if held, but the Bow is neither an ingredient nor a discount; failure leaves it intact. The Crossbow reloads slower than the Bow and should deal roughly 1.5–2× Bow damage as a tuning range, not a fixed ratio. Carry limits are 24 Bow Arrows and 16 Crossbow Bolts. Exact damage, reload times, bolt recipes/output, and the Stone-and-Wool Bow's visible construction remain **open**. The Bolt Pouch is a later idea in `IDEAS.md`.
- **Alchemist:** craft Wool-based Bandages. Each Bandage should restore approximately 15% of maximum HP (placeholder), and a player may carry at most three. Healing time, interruptions, cooldown, and recipe quantities remain **open**.
- **Carpenter/team projects:** the Battering Ram placeholder recipe is 18 Wood, 8 Stone, 4 Wool, and 2 Smelted Iron: 32 material units or 46 weighted load points. It can be brought in two overloaded 25-point trips by a motivated solo player, though gathering and travel may require more trips. Wood forms frame and log, Stone ballast, Wool canopy, and Iron striking head/brackets. The ram accepts shared contributions and refunds as below. A fixed defensive Ballista is also built by team construction, uses Fletcher-made bolts, and is funded from the vault. Exact ballista and shared gate/wall/tower repair recipes remain **open**.

## Castle and assault

- **Decided 2026-10-04:** each castle has one wall ring with identified sections, four corner towers, one gate with a separate door, a vault room inside the Keep, and team-colored banners. `layout.json` v2.2 owns the approved geometry: 183.6 × 183.6 ring, 35 wall sections, castle centers at x = ±420, inward-facing gates at x = ±328.2, and a 44 × 44 Keep with an 18 × 12 former stockpile room to serve as the vault room. The relationship between Keep shell damage and the vault target awaits clarification; the geometry is unchanged.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** defenders can stand on tower tops via stairs or ladders. Gate, wall sections, towers, and the vault have independent HP and intact/damaged/rubble visuals. Walls, gate, and towers can be repaired from team deposits with visible scaffolding; the vault cannot be repaired. Players can damage the vault with hand tools, swords, and arrows whenever they reach it. The ram damages only the gate, never the vault. Exact HP, weapon damage multipliers, repair rates/costs, and damage-state thresholds remain **open**.
- **Decided 2026-10-04:** the first playable has a barracks and four crafting specialists in separate village buildings: blacksmith, fletcher, carpenter, and alchemist. The blacksmith's forge replaces the shared workshop and smelts Ore; the Fletcher makes Bow, Crossbow, and ammunition; the Carpenter handles ram and other team construction; the Alchemist makes Bandages. NPCs craft only: no ranks, theft, negotiation, or trading. The barracks houses team spawns and a protected exit. The former stockpile room inside the Keep becomes the vault room, which accepts team deposits and is the physical win target. The approved room coordinates are in `layout.json` v2.2. Mason, cook, and weaver remain in `IDEAS.md`.
- **Gate route, revised 2026-10-04:** the widest entrance can be opened by heavy player hits or the battering ram, which damages the gate only. The ram is optional but should be pursued in most matches; target the first completed ram around minute 4. One active ram per team is the current target. It is a standing team project at the Carpenter: teammates contribute from carried goods or authorize vault-funded contributions, each source tracked for refunds. The ram appears once its full ingredient goal is met. After completion, multiple teammates may join or leave its moving/operating crew without losing their contribution record; the largest personal contributor still gets the first operator opportunity. Exact crew controls, claim window, ram damage, and counterplay are **open**. Defenders should be able to open their own gate; anti-troll access and enemy entry rules are **open** and must be settled before implementing a manual gate control.
- **Ram refunds:** players cannot cancel unfinished construction to use it as storage. If a game-defined event ends a project unfinished, return vault-sourced units to the vault. Return player-sourced units to each original contributor's bag up to its free weighted load capacity; place excess in an owner-only claim at the Carpenter. Only that contributor can collect what fits later. Claims cannot be spent or collected by teammates and clear at match reset. Which events close a project and whether overload capacity may be used for automatic refunds are **open**.
- **Ram operator priority:** the teammate with the largest direct personal contribution gets the first opportunity to operate a completed ram. Compare weighted load points donated directly; vault allocation grants no individual priority. A contributor who disconnects or leaves the ram crew cannot block use, and contribution credit persists for the match. The ram remains team equipment. Ties, claim-window duration, operator handoff, and how many teammates can push at once are **open**. EXP rewards can follow later.
- **Wall route, revised 2026-10-04:** destroying any wall section makes an entrance. Hand tools and other player weapons may damage walls, but doing so is deliberately inefficient. The battering ram exclusively damages the gate; it cannot strike wall sections. Other dedicated wall-breaching siege tools are planned for later design. A breach remains a gate door or wall section at 0 HP, even though the vault can also be reached by tunnel.
- **Tunnel route, revised 2026-10-04:** one mirrored L-shaped low-ceiling route per castle has a concealed outer entrance, long crawl leg, bend, and an exit in the forward courtyard away from the vault. It skips part of the open-field and gate fight, but attackers must cross defended courtyard space to reach the vault. It admits one crawler at a time, moves slower than open-ground travel, and cannot carry a ram. Crawlers cannot fight while inside. A defender at either entrance/exit can shoot down a straight section with a crossbow, while the bend breaks a full-length sightline; firing from inside the tunnel is not required. `layout.json` v2.2 owns current coordinates. Width, crawl speed, and travel time remain placeholders for avatar-scale validation.
- **Ballista, revised 2026-10-04:** the fixed castle ballista targets attackers and siege tools but must be built through a shared team construction project before use. A defender operates it manually, and each shot consumes a Ballista Bolt. Enemy attacks and siege damage can damage it; any teammate may authorize vault-funded construction/repair. Repair while it is under attack is disallowed. Its HP, firing arc, cadence, damage, ammunition delivery, construction recipe, and detailed repair rule remain **open**. The old trebuchet and cannons remain parked.

## Combat, movement, and onboarding (revised 2026-10-04)

- **Controls:** WASD moves; Shift sprints; Ctrl crouches; F blocks; left click swings. Crossbow firing/reload, interaction, and mobile equivalents need explicit mappings before implementation. Crouching must work behind crater rims and barricades at normal gameplay camera height.
- **First-playtest priority:** direct sword combat, basic ranged combat, sprint, crouch, and block are desired in the first playable when feasible. The user wants every named combat and movement mechanic eventually, but polish such as the armor equip animation and any unscoped dodge move must not block a playable siege. Exact stamina, block, damage, and animation timings remain open.
- **Barracks respawn:** a briefly protected spawn area must prevent enemy camping immediately after respawn. Protection duration, whether it ends on leaving or attacking, and how enemies are kept out need explicit rules before coding. This does not change the decided match-wide no-friendly-fire rule.
- **Onboarding:** the first playtest should communicate gathering, crafting, contribution, defending, and vault assault through visible world cues and contextual prompts. An introductory tutorial comes after observing real players' stuck points.

## Legacy concepts — parked until redesigned for two teams

The following details describe the old free-for-all game. Their code and notes may remain for reference. They are not instructions to build these mechanics into the current siege. The v1 blacksmith uses a dedicated forge; other specialist stations and buildings await the village-like interior revision. Old rank, theft, negotiation, and villager systems stay parked.

### Old specialists

Old roles were blacksmith/armorer, alchemist, carpenter, farmer/miner, and merchants. The former theft, ranks, and rebellious flag assumed player-owned villages.
- Roles: **blacksmith/armorer** (battle weapons), **alchemist** (research), **carpenter** (siege equipment like trebuchets, plus castle building), **farmer/miner** NPCs (classic resource gathering). **Merchants**: randomly-occurring near castles, kept deliberately abstract for now.
- Specialists have investable skill rankings.
- Stealable: thief gets the specialist at a flat 85% efficiency (not scaled by skill level).
- Rebellious flag (player-set, hidden from raiders): **currently unresolved.** As designed, it has no real cost, meaning flagging everyone rebellious is strictly optimal — needs a fix before building. Two live options:
  - **(A) Standing efficiency tax:** flagged specialist works at reduced output at all times, whether stolen or not — simple, no RNG needed.
  - **(B) Standing defection risk:** flagged specialist keeps full efficiency, but carries an ongoing small chance of defecting/sabotaging even with no raid involved — this is the version that actually uses "probabilistic," but creates a passive background-monitoring feel similar to the decay mechanic that was already deliberately rejected elsewhere, so weigh that tension before picking it.
  - Pick one before building the rebellious flag.
- Spy variant (rebellious specialist, alternate outcome): reveals the base layout and the specific building they're held in, aiding the original owner's counter-raid.

### Old negotiation and reputation

Parked design. Written for player-vs-player theft in free-for-all; in a two-team match the negotiating parties are teams, which changes most of this.
- Theft of a specialist triggers negotiation between the two players, capped at a fixed number of rounds. The messenger cannot be refused outright (theft is deliberate, engagement is forced).
- Either side can escalate by killing the messenger — this enrages the killer's own troops and damages their reputation.
- Hitting the round cap with no resolution costs the thief, treated similarly to killing the messenger.
- **Important scope correction:** reputation is now within-match only. The original design had it persistent across sessions with recency-weighted decay — that assumed a persistent world, which no longer exists post-pivot. Reputation effects (mercenary availability, etc.) should reset with each new match.
- Bait (deliberately under-defending a location to lure a raid) is a real, later-stage feature — needs the base raid loop solid first.

## Cut or parked
- Persistent, no-wipe world.
- Cross-session/persistent faction reputation.
- Administrative distance decay from a faction capital.
- Player-vassals with real autonomy who can defect between factions.
- Resource node degradation as a check on compact monopolies.
- Free-for-all and last-squad-standing; player-built bases and villages; folding eliminated players into another team; AI troop regiments.
- Old trebuchet, defensive cannons, movable siege cannon, and tunnel collapse. Existing assets and code may remain dormant.
- No cross-match dominance; within-match snowball is untested — playtest.
